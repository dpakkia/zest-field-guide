Visual identity

The baseline
CONTROLLED
ENERGY

Outside the counter: crowds, music, lights, movement, beer, festival. Inside the counter: precision, hands, ice, glass, metal, repetition, concentration.

Zest should not visually compete with the festival by being louder.
It should feel like the precise, elegant and controlled exception inside the energy.
Typography & title-mask treatment

Use an elegant but bold sans serif. Primary suggestion: Inter Tight ExtraBold / Black. Alternatives: Neue Haas Grotesk Display Bold or Helvetica Now Display Bold.

Titles are one meaningful word, uppercase, large, white or slightly warm white, with no outline or shadow. The foreground subject is masked so part of the word sits behind the person, hand, bottle, or tool.

Only roughly 15–30% of the letters should be occluded. It must read instantly. The title should exist for roughly 0.7–1.2 seconds, then disappear while the shot continues.

Whenever possible, use a locked or controlled shot with clean subject/background separation to make masking fast and elegant.

Cover system

The title shot is also the cover concept. After getting the moving shot, keep the composition and take a higher-resolution still/RAW photograph.

Keep the title and important subject within the central 3:4 profile-grid-safe region while composing the 9:16 master.

All three covers use the same grammar: human/action + large word + word partly behind subject. Same typography, different emotional state.

What should stay consistent later

Do not standardize a mandatory logo intro/outro. The identity is subtler: one-word title, subject-over-type masking, controlled composition, real sound, restrained slow motion, work rather than product, and quiet endings.

Later this can naturally become PRACTICE, SERVICE, STIR, POUR, BUILD, CLOSE, RESET, SHIFT, etc.

Creative direction

This is the purest statement of Zest’s identity: preparation as ritual. It should feel deliberate, tactile and almost documentary. Sound carries the edit.

Do not rush. Let isolated actions have weight. The moment the bar is ready, introduce the distant festival ambience as the world outside suddenly becoming audible.

Sound shape
silence THUD pause CLACK pause SCRAPE CHOP POP POUR CLACK SHHHHK festival ambience CUT

No need for a logo sting. The title, account, sound and visual grammar already identify the piece.

02 — PULSE

0 / 0
Rhythm / service
PULSE
Length20–25 s
VoiceNone
MusicService itself
StoryAction → rhythm → flow
Creative direction

This reel establishes Zest as the exception inside a beer festival. Begin with beer imagery, then interrupt it with a shaker impact. No explanatory text is needed.

The edit should gradually turn bar sounds into percussion. Later, stage bass, crowd and music can bleed into the rhythm until it becomes hard to tell whether the festival is driving the bar or the bar is generating the beat.

Rule: camera follows the work, not the drink. Cocktails are the result of the choreography, not the subject.
Sound shape
beer beer beer CLACK ice tin jigger glass shake glass tin shake + stage bass + crowd + applause + kick CUT one final CLACK

Rhythmic editing does not mean every shot must be 0.2 seconds. The long static wide is an intentional interruption that proves the coordination is real.

03 — AFTER

0 / 0
Complete cycle / aftermovie
AFTER
Length35–45 s
VoiceNone
MusicSparse / optional
StoryEmpty → alive → empty
Creative direction

This is not a generic festival aftermovie with some Zest shots. It is a film about operating a bar for five nights inside a festival.

The ending matters as much as the peak. Begin with emptiness, escalate through preparation and pressure, then deliberately decompress into cleanup, lights off and silence.

Do not structure it as Thursday / Friday / Saturday / Sunday / Monday. Build one emotional cycle instead.

Suggested time curve

0–8 s — Anticipation: empty, setup, lights, first customer.

8–25 s — Escalation: festival appears, crowd grows, bar accelerates.

25–32 s — Peak: densest service, stage, crowd, people, pressure.

32–42 s — Decompression: last order, cleanup, cloth, lights, empty.

Stories — nightly diary

0 / 0
15 curated observations / 5 nights
STORIES

Stories are not mini-Reels and not generic live updates. They are precise observations from a specific moment: short, polished, mostly single-shot or very lightly edited, and always meaningful.

Length6–12 s
VoiceNone
Text1 word / time
SoundReal first
Classy rule: show intensity without disorder. No rubbish, chaotic counters, exhausted “survival” imagery or deliberately ugly behind-the-scenes details. Energy comes from movement, sound, light, repetition and coordination.
Story visual grammar

Use the same bold sans-serif and subject-over-title mask language as the Reels when the frame supports it. Do not force the effect onto every Story.

Prefer one strong composition over a montage. If a Story uses cuts, every cut should have a sonic or conceptual reason.

No GIFs, stickers, polls or “come find us!” social clutter in this curated series. The Story itself should communicate the moment.

Stories = moments. Reels = constructions. If an 8-second real moment already works, preserve it instead of cutting it into Reel-style coverage.

Five-night arc
THU OPEN → LOUDER → NIGHT FRI RHYTHM → TEMPO → 00:00 SAT BEFORE → FLOW → LIVE SUN MIX → SYNC → MIDNIGHT MON READY → LAST → END

Read together, the Stories move from arrival to rhythm, service, musical intersection and closure without ever leaving Zest’s elegant visual world.

Shooting grammar

Frame rate
25 fps = realityWork, hands, service, people, wide shots, crowds, most bartending. Fast hands should look fast.
50 fps = attentionIce falling, citrus spray, garnish, liquid, one expression, one stage-light moment. Roughly 10–15% of footage maximum.
Lens language
24–35 mmEnvironment, choreography, reality, the whole system.
50–70 mmWork, hands, people, precise actions.
70–200 mmOccasional compressed stage/festival connection.

The wide locked shots are more important than endless macro B-roll. Watching multiple people coordinate in one frame communicates competence.

Camera movement
Locked = defaultLet action enter, cross and leave the frame.
Handheld = observationHuman moments, imperfection, team micro-behaviour.
Slow push = rare emphasisUse only when a moment actually deserves it.
Gimbal = almost noneNo orbiting cocktails or generic event floating.

If the subject already moves beautifully, the camera does not need to.

Editing rules

Sound creates transitions. Prefer hard cuts motivated by impacts, pours, wipes, opens and environmental changes.

Do not hide the beer. Beer is the context that makes Zest’s difference legible. The beer cocktail can visually bridge the two worlds.

Work over product. Follow hands, coordination, coordination, resets, repetition. The drink is the consequence.

Quiet endings. Avoid ending every piece at the peak. Shutdown, wipe, darkness and residual ambience are part of the identity.

Cover compositions

PREPARE: bartender alone, relatively clean space, body slightly off-centre, one arm intersecting the title. Spacious and controlled.

PULSE: peak service, multiple depth planes, foreground bartender crossing the typography while background work stays visible. Busy image, simple type.

AFTER: most human/environmental. One bartender or team member foreground, stage/crowd behind, darker and more nocturnal.
